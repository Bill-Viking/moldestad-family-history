# Ole Olsen Moldestad

Also recorded as Ola Olsson Moldestad and O. O. Moldestad. Person ID: **MOL-001**.

**Working dates:** August 11, 1834 – December 9, 1905. The birth date is a reading of the original register; the death date is reported by a later history. The qualifications below matter.

## Norway

Ole came from Moldestad in Breim, Gloppen, Nordfjord. The original Gloppen baptism register, page 34, entry 39, appears to give his birth as **August 11, 1834**, with baptism on August 20. His parents are Ole Jacobsen Moldestad and Anne Jonsdatter. This is a handwriting reading, not a certified transcription. An earlier index reading gave August 1; Gimmestad's later history gives August 3, 1835, and Davidson gives the year 1835. The 1871 marriage record's 1834 birth year supports the original-register reading. [S01, S05, S07, S08](../sources/README.md)

The retained transcription of his parents' marriage gives June 21, 1812. His mother's name also appears as Anna Jonsdatter, associated with Kleppe. The name variants alone do not establish different people. The possible connection to Jacob Christianssen and Mari Olsdatter through the 1801 and 1815 censuses remains a research lead. [S02, S03](../sources/README.md)

In the 1865 census, a matching Ole Olsen appears at Bersæter/Bergset as an unmarried lodger and **cooper**. His name, calculated birth year, birthplace and location make this a strong match, although the identification still depends on comparing records. Andrine Villumsdatter, later his wife, appears on the same property in a different household. The census does not establish when they first met. [S04](../sources/README.md)

## Marriage and migration

Ole Olsen and Andrine Villumsdatter married on **March 28, 1871**. The retained marriage transcription and original image agree; their fathers are named as Ole Jacobsen and Villum Andersen. [S05](../sources/README.md)

The Norway Heritage passenger transcription places Ole, age 36, and an adjacent entry for Andrine, age 30, on the **Argo**, leaving Bergen on **May 14, 1871** and reaching Quebec on **June 26**. The original passenger list has not yet been independently checked for this project. Those dates place departure 47 days after the marriage and give a crossing of 43 elapsed days. Davidson's later account instead says they married a year before sailing and spent eleven weeks at sea. The dated records are preferred provisionally, with the disagreement retained. [S05, S06, S08](../sources/README.md)

## Building a life in Minnesota

A. A. Davidson's undated *Early Days in Our Community* describes the couple's arrival through Quebec and Mankato. According to that account, Ole worked moving baggage between a hotel and the depot for a dollar a day plus his keep. Nellie went on toward the Hegdahl family through Golden Gate. After accompanying her and finding his hotel job gone on his return, Ole worked in the harvest fields until he had earned $35, then walked back to the settlement. These are later recollections, not payroll or travel records. [S08](../sources/README.md)

Davidson describes a forty-acre purchase, a log house, river work and railroad work. Gimmestad also associates the family's early forty acres with Swedes Forest. The sources do not establish that the farm cost $35 or that this was all the money Ole possessed. Their independence from one another has not been established. [S07, S08](../sources/README.md)

The histories place the family near Delhi in **1879**. Davidson describes a quarter-section purchase north of Delhi and reports that Ole held 400 acres at his death. Deeds and probate records are still needed to test the acreage, dates and means of acquisition. Larger acreages reported elsewhere for William and Abraham concern his sons, not additional holdings automatically attributable to Ole. [S07, S08](../sources/README.md)

## Family and later years

The histories name Anna or Anne, William, Abraham, Maria and Maline or Lina as children. Davidson also reports two children who died in infancy without supplying their names. Anna's reported 1866 birth predates the couple's 1871 marriage; her baptism is needed to establish the relationship directly. See the [family record](../families/ole-and-nellie.md). [S07, S08](../sources/README.md)

Gimmestad gives Ole's death as **December 9, 1905**; Davidson agrees on the year. Davidson describes Ole and Nellie as charter members of Rock Dell Church and says they were buried there. Primary death and church records remain to be located. [S07, S08](../sources/README.md)

## What the evidence suggests

The cooper's occupation and the later accounts of wage work and farming suggest a man able to use different skills as circumstances changed. They do not reveal his reasons for emigrating, his politics, his temperament or his private hopes. The landholding story needs records before it can support conclusions about inheritance, debt or wealth.

## Records that would help

- The original Argo passenger list and Norwegian emigration records.
- Minnesota death, cemetery and Rock Dell Church records.
- Deeds and probate for Swedes Forest and Delhi.
- Anna's baptism and primary links to the proposed grandparents.
