# Abraham / Abe Moldestad

Minnesota farmer and landholder, also identified with photography in Delhi.

**Working dates:** January 25, 1874 or 1875 – February 1968. The birth year is unresolved; the death month comes from a secondary genealogy index.

[Share a record or correction](https://github.com/Bill-Viking/moldestad-family-history/discussions) · [His parents' family](../families/ole-and-nellie.md) · [Sources](../sources/README.md)

## Family and beginnings

Abraham, known as Abe, was a son of [Ole Olsen Moldestad](ole-moldestad.md) and [Nille Andrine / Nellie Bergset Moldestad](nellie-moldestad.md). Gimmestad's 1940 history gives his birth in Swedes Forest on **January 25, 1874**. A later genealogy index gives **January 25, 1875**. The day and month agree, but a primary birth or church record is needed to settle the year. [S07, S08, A01](../sources/README.md)

His parents had emigrated from Norway in 1871. Later histories describe the family's early farming in Swedes Forest and its move to the Delhi area in 1879. Ole's hotel work, harvest earnings, river work and first land purchase belong to the father's story; they are not episodes from Abe's own working life. [S07, S08](../sources/README.md)

Abe married **Jennie E. Lyslo**, whom Davidson associates with Benson. Davidson reports four children. Gimmestad prints March 31, 1888 as the marriage date, but a later index gives that date as Jennie's birth. The printed wedding date is therefore not accepted here, and no replacement date is invented. [S07, S08, A01](../sources/README.md)

## Farming and land

Gimmestad reports **800 acres** for Abraham. Davidson's later, undated account says he had accumulated several farms and retired. These reports support a picture of substantial landholding and eventual economic security. They do not establish which properties he purchased, inherited, worked himself or rented to others. Deeds, mortgages and probate records would help distinguish those possibilities. [S07, S08](../sources/README.md)

The family's photographs include a farm view labeled as Abe's, with agricultural buildings and cattle. Such an image can describe a scene, but it does not establish acreage, boundaries or who built each structure. Another house photograph has an inscription that may identify a Maurstad property; its filename alone is not enough to call it Abe's own home. [A02](../sources/README.md)

## Photography

One labeled photograph places Abe beside a sign identifying a **Moldestad photographer in Delhi, Minnesota**. The archive identifies the subject as Abe. Together, the inscription and family label are strong evidence of his connection to photography. A directory, advertisement or business record is still needed to establish dates, premises and the nature of the business. [A02](../sources/README.md)

A separate youthful portrait has a Redwood Falls studio imprint. That identifies the studio that made the portrait; it does not establish that Abe took it. Other family photographs cannot be attributed to him without a photographer's mark or further evidence. [A02](../sources/README.md)

## Activities and community

A hunting photograph has a reverse identifying William and Abe. Several scans preserve the same scene, so they are evidence of one photographed occasion rather than several outings. The date and frequency of their hunting remain unknown. Other labeled pictures place Abe at family gatherings and near livestock. A car in a group photograph does not, by itself, prove his ownership. [A02](../sources/README.md)

Gimmestad's Nordfjord memorial-gift donor list records a **$5 contribution from Abe** and a separate $5 contribution from William. This documents participation in a charitable effort for people in need in Nordfjord. It does not establish an office, a recurring commitment or the full extent of his giving. [S07](../sources/README.md)

## A notebook with uncertain authorship

A notebook described in the archive as found in Abe's farmhouse contains farm accounts and cattle-breeding entries. One page records expenses of $16.95, receipts of $16.32 and a loss of $0.63. Another contains breeding and expected calving dates in 1933–1934. The handwriting's author and the notebook's ownership have not been established. These figures must not be treated as Abe's entire farm accounts, an annual loss or his personal diary. [A03](../sources/README.md)

## Later years and interpretation

A secondary index places Abe's death in **February 1968** and Jennie's in **August 1977**. These dates remain leads pending primary confirmation. [A01](../sources/README.md)

The evidence suggests practical and entrepreneurial breadth: farming, reported ownership of several farms, and a photography connection. Financial security is a reasonable inference from the reported retirement. His own hopes remain unknown because no personal letter or diary expressing them has been identified in the research reviewed so far.

The available material does not justify conclusions about his temperament, politics, marital relationships or treatment of employees. Nor does it establish that he built his holdings without inherited resources. A detailed life can acknowledge those gaps without filling them with imagined motives.

## Records that would help

- A primary birth or baptism record resolving 1874 versus 1875.
- Abe and Jennie's marriage record and primary death records.
- Delhi directories, advertisements or records of the photography business.
- Deeds and estate records testing the reported 800 acres.
- Evidence of the notebook's author and dates for labeled photographs.
